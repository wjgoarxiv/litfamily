We assigned 84 first-year biology students to one of two groups. For six weeks, the practice group completed four 20-minute reading sessions each week; the comparison group kept its usual study schedule.

In week seven, students took a 30-question quiz. The practice group averaged 21.4 correct answers, compared with 18.7 in the comparison group. Both groups had similar scores on the 10-question baseline quiz.

The results suggest that regular reading may improve recall, though further research is needed. The study followed 84 students for six weeks. Future studies can test whether the pattern holds in other groups.
